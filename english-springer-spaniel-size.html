How Big Do English Springer Spaniels Get? Owner-Reported Weights and Heights, Working vs Show Lines

How big do English Springer Spaniels get? The English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association describes mature females at about 19 inches at the shoulder and 40 lb and males at about 20 inches and 50 lb. Those are breed references. The owner reports below show how much individual dogs can differ, including within the working line.

What size does the U.S. parent club describe?

The club describes an adult female at roughly 19 inches and 40 pounds, and an adult male at roughly 20 inches and 50 pounds. Metric conversions are rounded here for easy comparison.

MetricValueSource
Female shoulder height19 in (48.3 cm)ESSFTA
Male shoulder height20 in (50.8 cm)ESSFTA
Female weight40 lb (18.1 kg)ESSFTA
Male weight50 lb (22.7 kg)ESSFTA

1 inch and 10 pounds separate the club’s two reference points. The weight difference applies to those reference figures and should not be added to the weight of an individual female to predict a male’s size.

Think of the table as two paired descriptions. When comparing your scale reading, keep your dog’s actual frame and shoulder measurement beside it. If you’re keeping notes while your puppy grows, put the dates of those measurements beside your observations from the puppy coat transition. The visual change in the coat and the measurements are separate observations you can track.

How do working and show Springers compare?

Show Bred English Springer Spaniel describes show dogs as generally larger and more substantially boned and working dogs as generally finer in bone. The weight records show why this description works better as a broad comparison than as a cutoff.

If you’re looking at photos of prospective parents, ask for each dog’s age, measured shoulder height and recent weight. A side view can help you understand the outline. What do the measured heights tell us?

The paired reports include a working female at 18 inches and two dogs whose breeding lines were unreported. They show the value of recording height and weight together. They leave the question of average height by line open.

ExampleReported sizeSource
Bramble, working female, 15 months18 in and about 16 kgHorse & Hound
Otto, male, age and line unreported21 in at shoulder and 22 kgHorse & Hound
Male, 4½ years, line unreported49 cm and 24 kgHorse & Hound

49 cm is about 19.3 in. The owner gave a height and weight but no measurement method. Otto’s owner specified the shoulder and described the dog as needing to gain weight. Their numbers can illustrate size without setting weight goals.

For a repeatable home measurement, stand your dog on level flooring with the feet planted and the head in a natural position. Find the withers where the shoulder blades meet the top line. Hold a rigid straightedge level over that point and note the distance from its underside to the floor. Repeat after the dog has stepped away and stood again. If the readings differ, record both and check your setup before treating the change as growth.

For a car crate, take fresh measurements for the specific manufacturer’s sizing instructions and measure the opening it has to pass through. The shoulder number answers a breed-size question. Your dog’s standing clearance and the dimensions of your vehicle answer the fit question. See our Springer car setup guide for planning the crate and its surroundings.

How were the owner reports selected?

We selected 11 individual size reports across two public threads to illustrate differences within and between lines. The examples include dogs with an age and weight when those details were supplied, and separate examples with a paired weight and height. We kept line labels as supplied by owners, translated “field” to “working” and “bench” to “show,” and used “unspecified” where the owner gave no line. Known crosses and puppy-only weights were left out.

These records are self-reported. We did not inspect pedigrees, verify body condition or measure any dogs. The sample was purposive and the groups have different age and sex mixes. Averages from these rows would mostly describe which examples we included. One owner’s weight interval is preserved rather than collapsed to a single value. Converted values are rounded to one decimal, and a conversion does not make the original reading more precise.

Frequently asked questions

How big is an adult English Springer Spaniel?

The U.S. parent club gives approximate adult figures of 19 inches at the shoulder and 40 pounds for females, and 20 inches and 50 pounds for males. Those figures are a size reference. For a crate or harness, measure the dog you are fitting.

Are working Springers always smaller than show Springers?

There is overlap in the owner reports. The owner of Nacho, a field Springer, gave a range of 48–50 pounds. One bench male was reported at 48 pounds. That is enough to show why a single weight cannot tell you which breeding line a dog comes from.

How tall were the owner-reported Springers?

In the selected height reports, Bramble was reported at 18 inches and Otto at 21 inches. Another owner listed a height of 49 cm, about 19.3 inches. No selected show-line report included a height, so these examples cannot estimate the height difference between working and show dogs.

Do you measure Springer height to the head?

For a breed comparison, measure from the floor to the withers, the highest point around the shoulder blades. For clearance under a solid roof, measure the dog in the posture it will use to enter and stand in the space. Those two measurements answer different questions.

Does the size checker tell me what my Springer should weigh?

The checker keeps breed-club reference figures and individual reports visible. It converts measurements and filters reports by the line the owner named. It cannot judge an individual dog’s body condition or predict a puppy’s full-grown size from a current reading.

Use the breed figures to picture a Springer’s size and the owner reports to see the variety within that picture. When you need an exact measurement, take it from your dog and write down how you measured it.
